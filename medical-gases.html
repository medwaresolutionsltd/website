Medical Gases in Kenya: Hospital Systems, Supply & Installation Guide

A practical guide to medical gas pipeline systems, oxygen supply options, ISO 7396-1 installation and maintenance for hospitals and clinics in Kenya.

Medware Editorial Team Experts in Biomedical Engineering & Healthcare ICT

In short: medical gases in Kenyan hospitals are delivered through a medical gas pipeline system (MGPS) that carries oxygen, medical air, surgical air, nitrous oxide and vacuum from central plant to outlets at the bedside or theatre. The right supply source, whether cylinder manifold, PSA oxygen plant or bulk liquid, depends on your demand, space and continuity needs.

What Are Medical Gases?

Medical gases support treatment, anaesthesia, respiratory care and surgical procedures. They are essential in operating theatres, ICUs, maternity wards and emergency departments. The gases and services most often found in Kenyan hospitals are:

  • Medical oxygen: for respiratory support, emergency care, ICU and anaesthesia. Supplied by cylinders, PSA plants or bulk liquid systems.
  • Medical air: clean compressed air for ventilators and respiratory equipment.
  • Surgical air: higher-pressure air that powers surgical tools. It has a different specification from medical air and is not for breathing.
  • Nitrous oxide: used in selected anaesthesia and pain management.
  • Medical vacuum: the suction service needed for clinical procedures.

The exact mix depends on the facility's clinical services. A small clinic needs a very different setup from a tertiary hospital.

Why Reliable Medical Gas Systems Matter

A properly designed MGPS reduces cylinder handling, improves efficiency and supports patient safety through gas-specific outlets, isolation valves and alarm systems. It also lets a hospital add beds, theatres and ICU capacity later without a major redesign.

In Kenya, dependable oxygen delivery remains a top priority for many facilities that face supply or logistics challenges. Moving from scattered cylinder deliveries to a centrally managed system is one of the most effective ways to improve continuity of care.

Common Medical Gas Problems in Kenyan Hospitals

  • Unreliable oxygen supply: especially where facilities depend heavily on cylinder deliveries.
  • Low pipeline pressure: caused by undersized systems, high demand or equipment faults.
  • Leaks and ageing infrastructure: leading to wastage and safety risks.
  • Faulty alarms or missing backup: delaying the response when primary supply fails.
  • Inadequate maintenance: increasing the chance of unexpected breakdowns.

Most of these problems can be prevented with correct design, quality installation and a planned maintenance programme.

Main Components of a Medical Gas Pipeline System

A complete medical gas pipeline system typically includes:

  • Supply sources: cylinder manifolds, PSA oxygen plants, medical air compressors and vacuum plants.
  • Copper pipelines: medical-grade, correctly identified and sized. See our guide to medical gas copper pipes and ISO 7396-1 standards.
  • Area valve service units (AVSUs): for isolating a ward or theatre safely.
  • Gas-specific terminal units: outlets at the bedside or theatre that only accept the correct gas.
  • Alarm and monitoring panels: to warn staff of low pressure or source changeover.
  • Bedhead units and medical pendants: where required by the clinical area.

Medical Oxygen and Gas Supply Options in Kenya

There is no single best solution. The right choice depends on demand, space, utilities and continuity needs:

  • Cylinder manifold systems: practical for smaller facilities, and a dependable backup for larger ones.
  • PSA oxygen generating plants: ideal for hospitals with steady, predictable demand that want on-site production and less cylinder logistics.
  • Bulk liquid oxygen: suitable for high-volume facilities with proper site conditions and delivery access.
  • Hybrid arrangements: a primary plant combined with a cylinder reserve for higher resilience.
Tip: Always size the system for peak simultaneous demand and plan for future expansion. Generic capacity figures are rarely accurate for a specific hospital.

Medical Gas Installation Process

Proper installation is critical for safety and performance. A typical project follows these steps:

  1. Site assessment and clinical requirements definition
  2. System design and coordination with architectural and MEP teams
  3. Procurement of specified equipment and materials
  4. Pipeline and plant installation by qualified personnel
  5. Testing for leakage, pressure, flow, gas identity and alarms
  6. Commissioning, documentation and staff orientation
  7. Handover with maintenance recommendations

Medical Gas Maintenance and Safety

Even a well-installed system needs regular care. Key activities include preventive servicing of plants and manifolds, leak and pressure checks, alarm testing and keeping accurate maintenance records. Any work that affects supply must be coordinated with clinical teams to protect patients.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which medical oxygen supply is best for hospitals in Kenya?

It depends on demand, available space, power supply and continuity requirements. Cylinder manifolds, PSA oxygen plants and bulk liquid oxygen each have their place, and many hospitals combine a primary source with a backup. An engineering assessment of your peak demand is the safest starting point.

What is a medical gas pipeline system (MGPS)?

A medical gas pipeline system is a fixed network that delivers oxygen, medical air, surgical air, nitrous oxide and vacuum from central sources to terminal units at the point of care. It includes supply plant, pipework, isolation valves, outlets and alarm panels, and is designed to ISO 7396-1.

Does Medware install and maintain medical gas systems in Kenya?

Yes. Medware Solutions handles design support, supply, installation, testing, commissioning and ongoing maintenance of medical gas pipeline systems and related plant for hospitals across Kenya.

How often should medical gas systems be serviced?

Follow the manufacturer's recommendations and applicable standards. Critical plant such as oxygen generators, compressors, vacuum pumps and alarm panels should be inspected and serviced at defined intervals, with full records kept.

Is medical air the same as surgical air?

No. Medical air is breathable air for patients and ventilators. Surgical air is a higher-pressure supply that drives surgical tools and is not for breathing. They use separate pipelines and outlets with different specifications.

Can a PSA oxygen plant replace oxygen cylinders completely?

A correctly sized PSA plant can cover most of a hospital's routine oxygen demand, but a reserve supply such as a cylinder manifold is still recommended so that patients are protected during plant maintenance, power problems or faults.
