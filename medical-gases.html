Medical Gases in Kenya: Hospital Systems, Supply & Installation Guide

A practical guide to medical gas pipeline systems, oxygen supply options, installation standards and maintenance for hospitals and clinics in Kenya.

Reliable Medical Gases for Kenyan Hospitals

Medical gases are essential for patient care in operating theatres, ICUs, maternity wards and emergency departments. A well-designed medical gas pipeline system (MGPS) delivers oxygen, medical air, surgical air and vacuum safely and continuously to every point of care.

This guide covers the main gases, system components, supply options, installation and maintenance — and how Medware Solutions supports hospitals across Kenya.

What Are Medical Gases?

Medical gases support treatment, anaesthesia, respiratory care and surgical procedures. Common gases and services in Kenyan hospitals include:

  • Medical Oxygen — For respiratory support, emergency care, ICU and anaesthesia. Supplied via cylinders, PSA plants or bulk liquid systems.
  • Medical Air — Clean compressed air for ventilators and respiratory equipment.
  • Surgical Air — Powers surgical tools (different specification from medical air).
  • Nitrous Oxide — Used in selected anaesthesia and pain management.
  • Medical Vacuum — Essential suction service for clinical procedures.

The exact mix depends on the facility’s clinical services. A small clinic needs a different setup from a tertiary hospital.

Why Reliable Medical Gas Systems Matter

A properly designed MGPS reduces cylinder handling, improves efficiency and supports patient safety through gas-specific outlets, isolation valves and alarm systems. It also allows hospitals to plan for expansion of beds, theatres and ICUs without major redesign later.

In Kenya, dependable oxygen delivery remains a top priority for many facilities facing supply or logistics challenges.

Common Problems in Kenyan Hospitals

  • Unreliable oxygen supply — Especially where facilities depend heavily on cylinder deliveries.
  • Low pipeline pressure — Caused by undersized systems, high demand or equipment faults.
  • Leaks and ageing infrastructure — Leading to wastage and safety risks.
  • Faulty alarms or missing backup — Delaying response when primary supply fails.
  • Inadequate maintenance — Increasing the chance of unexpected breakdowns.

Most of these issues can be prevented with correct design, quality installation and a planned maintenance programme.

Main Components of a Medical Gas System

A complete medical gas pipeline system typically includes:

  • Supply sources (cylinder manifolds, PSA oxygen plants, medical air compressors, vacuum plants)
  • Copper pipelines with correct identification and sizing
  • Area valve service units for isolation
  • Gas-specific terminal units (outlets) at the bedside or theatre
  • Alarm and monitoring panels
  • Bedhead units or medical pendants where required

Medware Solutions supplies and installs these components to ISO 7396-1 standards. See our Medical Gas Systems page for the full range.

Medical Gas Supply Options in Kenya

There is no single best solution — the right choice depends on demand, space, utilities and continuity needs:

  • Cylinder manifold systems — Practical for smaller facilities or as backup.
  • PSA oxygen generating plants — Ideal for hospitals with steady, predictable demand that want on-site production and reduced cylinder logistics.
  • Bulk liquid oxygen — Suitable for high-volume facilities with proper site conditions and delivery access.
  • Hybrid arrangements — Combining a primary plant with cylinder backup for higher resilience.
Tip: Always size the system for peak simultaneous demand and plan for future expansion. Generic capacity figures are rarely accurate for a specific hospital.

Medical Gas Installation Process

Proper installation is critical for safety and performance. A typical project follows these steps:

  1. Site assessment and clinical requirements definition
  2. System design and coordination with architectural/MEP teams
  3. Procurement of specified equipment and materials
  4. Pipeline and plant installation by qualified personnel
  5. Testing (leakage, pressure, flow, gas identity, alarms)
  6. Commissioning, documentation and staff orientation
  7. Handover with maintenance recommendations

Medware Solutions manages the full process from design support through to commissioning and training.

Maintenance and Safety

Even a well-installed system needs regular care. Key activities include preventive servicing of plants and manifolds, leak and pressure checks, alarm testing, and keeping accurate maintenance records. Any work that affects supply must be coordinated with clinical teams to protect patients.
